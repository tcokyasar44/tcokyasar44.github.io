At a glance

I am an operations researcher who builds large-scale optimization models and algorithms for transportation, logistics, and energy systems: EV charging and fleet electrification, transit, freight, drone delivery, and shared mobility. I spent seven years at Argonne National Laboratory turning these methods into national- and metropolitan-scale studies for the U.S. Department of Energy, and I now direct the ATLAS Lab at Texas A&M University, which I co-founded with my ETID colleague Dr. Sudipta Chowdhury.

Research

Themes, methods & funding →

How should a city place chargers for thousands of delivery vans? How can a transit agency move to electric buses without buying more of them? I answer questions like these by combining mathematical programming, decomposition, and high-performance computing with agent-based simulation.

EV charging & electrification

Where to build chargers, how many, and how fleets should use them, solved at metropolitan and national scale with decomposition and high-performance computing.

Read more →

Transit & electric bus fleets

Scheduling, charger siting, and fleet transition for battery-electric and mixed electric-diesel bus systems, from single depots to multi-agency case studies.

Read more →

Freight & supply chains

Supplier selection, truckload markets, and urban e-commerce delivery, calibrated inside national freight simulations.

Read more →

Drones & last-mile delivery

Drone networks with battery swapping, drone–truck energy comparisons, and exact algorithms for mixed drone–ground fleets.

Read more →

Shared, autonomous & multimodal mobility

Ride-matching, shared autonomous vehicles, and transit redesign, evaluated with the POLARIS agent-based simulator.

Read more →

New directions: AI for optimization

Combining AI with optimization so that planners can ask what-if questions and get explainable answers; resilient logistics networks.

Short bio for introductions (third person, for event organizers)

Taner Cokyasar is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Engineering Technology and Industrial Distribution at Texas A&M University, where he directs the Advanced Transportation Logistics & AI for Resilient Systems (ATLAS) Lab, which he co-founded with Dr. Sudipta Chowdhury, and a Research Affiliate of the Texas A&M Transportation Institute. His research develops large-scale optimization models and algorithms for transportation, logistics, and energy systems, including EV charging, transit and fleet scheduling, freight, drone delivery, and shared mobility. Before joining Texas A&M, he spent seven years at Argonne National Laboratory, most recently as a Computational Transportation Engineer, and he has led four U.S. Department of Energy awards through Argonne as principal investigator. He is President of the IISE Engineering Economy Division and an Area Editor of The Engineering Economist. He holds M.S. and Ph.D. degrees in Industrial Engineering from the University of Tennessee, Knoxville.
